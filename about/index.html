Jake's story

Twenty-five years on the ladder, and what I did when I stepped off it.

The first act

For twenty-five years I did what you're supposed to do. I climbed the ladder, took the promotions, built the credentials, and raised a family. By my late forties I had all the markers of a successful career and a week I didn't recognize as my own. I was working more than fifty hours, a great deal of it on things that didn't need me, and I hadn't built the kind of independence I had always assumed would simply arrive.

The turning point

At 48 I stopped assuming. I worked out what financial independence would actually take for someone starting late, and I began building toward it alongside my job. I logged a full week of my own time and didn't like what I saw, so I started cutting the work that should never have existed. When AI tools arrived I learned them from the first day, not to become a technician but to get hours back. None of it was quick, and I made most of the mistakes my books now warn you about.

Where I am now

I'm 54. I'm financially independent, my day job is optional, and my time is my own. I'm married with kids, and I live an ordinary life with most of the pressure taken out of it. I write, I keep learning, and I'm around for the people I care about. That's the whole of it, and it's a great deal more than I had at 48.

What I believe

The rules change at 50, and nobody tells you. The trade that made sense at 30, your time for security, stops making sense once you have some security and are running short of time. I don't think age is a liability. I think it's the point at which you finally know enough to choose differently, and most people don't act on that.

I think AI is a tool for taking your time back, not a threat to wait for. I think success at this age stops meaning titles and starts meaning options. And I think a real life beats a perfect one. I'm not writing to you from a beach. I have responsibilities like everyone else, and the books are about working within them.

Why I write

Men my age tend to get stuck in one of three places. Some are still chasing the next rung on a ladder that no longer leads anywhere they want to go. Some are afraid of becoming irrelevant. Some have quietly started counting down. I've been in all three. These books are field notes on a fourth option, which is to design a different game.

I'm not a guru, and I don't have every answer. I write down what worked for me, what didn't, and what you might try, with the tools and the steps included. I'm also not a licensed professional of any kind, which is why every page of this site says so at the bottom.

How the series works

The books are organized around seven pillars: money, time, health, career, relationships, AI and technology, and lifestyle. Each pillar opens with a foundation book, and shorter, more focused books follow it. Every book stands on its own, so you can begin wherever your biggest problem is. If you're not sure where that is, start here.
