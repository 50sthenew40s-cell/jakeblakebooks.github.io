The Financial Architect

How to Build Wealth When You're Running Out of Time

This book is not financial advice. Talk to a qualified financial professional before you make investment decisions.

Most financial advice assumes you started at 25. This book starts at 50.

The standard plan says to start investing at 25, be patient, and retire at 65. If you're around 50 and that isn't how it went, the standard plan has very little to say to you. You can't save your way to independence in fifteen years on a normal salary.

The Financial Architect takes a different route: work out your own financial independence number, build more than one stream of income while you still have your job, and automate the parts that would otherwise rely on willpower. The book is frank about what that costs. Its own timeline is eight to twelve years, and it tells you that the first two are the hardest and that most people stop before the results show.

The numbers in it are deliberately modest. They're there so that you can see how the method works, and the book treats them as examples.

What's inside

  1. Part 1: Calculate Your NumberHow to work out your own financial independence number, so that you're aiming at a specific figure and not a vague idea of enough.
  2. Part 2: The Income Streams That Actually WorkFive streams with honest timelines: dividend investing, freelancing, content creation, online courses, and affiliate marketing.
  3. Part 3: Tools for Automation and AIThe tools that track your money, automate your investing, and handle invoicing while you still have a day job.
  4. Part 4: Real-World ScenariosThree starting points with different incomes and savings, and what the timeline looks like for each.
  5. Part 5: Common MistakesThe mistakes that derail most attempts, from chasing exotic investments and timing the market to quitting too early.
  6. Part 6: Your 90-Day Action PlanA phase-by-phase plan that takes you from reading the book to running the system.

Is this book for you?

It's for you if

  • You're around 50 and feel behind on retirement.
  • You have a steady income and still can't save your way there.
  • You're willing to do steady, unglamorous work for years.

It isn't for you if

  • You want to be rich in a year or two.
  • You're looking for crypto, options trading, or schemes.
  • You want someone to tell you what to buy.

Tools used in this book

The tools in the book are free or cheap on purpose.

  • A brokerage account that can invest automatically (the book uses Fidelity as its example)
  • Wave, for invoicing side work
  • A simple tracking spreadsheet
  • An AI assistant such as Claude or ChatGPT

Questions readers ask

Is this financial advice?
No. Jake is not a licensed financial advisor. The book explains a framework and shows examples, and it tells you to consult a qualified professional before you make investment decisions.
How long does the book say this takes?
Eight to twelve years of consistent work. It says plainly that the first two years are the hardest and that most people quit before the results become visible.
What does it mean by income streams?
Five of them: dividend investing, freelancing, content creation, online courses, and affiliate marketing. The book warns against starting all five at once.
Is it too late to start at 50?
The book's whole argument is that it isn't, provided you use a method built for a fifteen-year runway instead of a forty-year one.
What happens after the first 90 days?
You keep going. The 90-day plan gets the system running, and the Final Notes describe what the first five years tend to feel like, including the long stretch when little seems to happen.
