The Time Architect

How to Reclaim Your Life by Eliminating What Doesn't Matter

You have everything at 50 except time. This is how you get it back.

You've tried getting up earlier, a better app, and an empty inbox, and the week filled right back up. That's because productivity advice is written for people who want to do more. At 50, with twenty-five years of commitments stacked on your calendar, you don't need to do more. You need less on your plate.

The Time Architect starts with a measurement. You log one honest week, sort every hour into four categories, and work out your Time Number: the hours per week you intend to take back, and what you'll use them for. Then you take each item through four decisions, always in the same order, and carry the result out over ninety days.

It's a workbook as much as a book. Every part ends with an exercise, and the examples follow three men with very different weeks from their first audit to their second.

What's inside

  1. Part 1: Calculate Your Time NumberRun a seven-day audit, sort every hour into four categories, and set a specific number of hours to take back, with a purpose for each one.
  2. Part 2: The Four DecisionsCut, automate, delegate, protect, in that order, with word-for-word scripts for leaving a meeting, stopping a report, and saying no.
  3. Part 3: The Five Time ThievesEmail and messages, meetings, other people's priorities, the phone and screens, and life admin. Each gets a fix, a free tool, and an exercise.
  4. Part 4: Tools for Automation and AIA tool setup in three layers, and a library of copy-and-paste prompts for replies, meetings, paperwork, and weekly planning.
  5. Part 5: Real-World ScenariosThree men and three very different weeks, followed through ninety days, including what didn't go to plan.
  6. Part 6: Common MistakesThe eight ways a plan like this falls apart, and how to catch each one in a week instead of a year.
  7. Part 7: Your 90-Day Action PlanFour phases, two routines, and a second audit that shows you in hours what you got back.

Is this book for you?

It's for you if

  • You work a full week and can't name what mattered in it.
  • Your calendar is run by other people's meetings and requests.
  • You carry obligations for your kids, a parent, or both.
  • You want a method with steps, not a pep talk.

It isn't for you if

  • You want to get more done in a day.
  • You're hoping an app will fix it for you.
  • You're looking for overnight change.

Tools used in this book

Every tool in the book is free or has a free plan that does the job.

  • Toggl Track or a Google Sheet, for the time audit
  • Google Calendar and Google Tasks
  • Gmail filters and templates
  • Calendly, for a booking link
  • Fathom, for meeting notes
  • An AI assistant such as Claude or ChatGPT
  • Zapier, for simple automation after day 90

Questions readers ask

How is this different from a productivity book?
Productivity books help you do more. This one helps you decide what shouldn't be on your plate at all. Its first rule is that nothing gets made faster until it has been tested for cutting.
How many hours will I get back?
The book doesn't promise a figure. You calculate your own number from your audit, and for the first ninety days it's capped at a quarter of the hours you could reclaim. The three examples in the book finish at 11, 10, and 6 hours a week, and they are illustrations, not guarantees.
Do I need to be good with technology?
No. The core tools are a calendar, a task list, a time tracker, and one AI assistant. The prompts are written so that you can copy them and fill in the blanks.
I don't have an assistant or a team. Can I still delegate?
Yes. The book covers four places to hand work to without any staff: an AI assistant, colleagues, your household, and the people the work really belongs to.
How long does the plan take?
Ninety days. Two weeks to measure, about two weeks to cut, a month to set up tools and hand things over, and a month to protect what you've won and measure again.
